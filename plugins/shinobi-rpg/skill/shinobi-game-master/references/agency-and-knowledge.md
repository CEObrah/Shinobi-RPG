# Agency and Knowledge

Use this reference when player choice, consent, authority, information access, recognition, allegiance, surrender, lethal intent, family commitment, or NPC independence could be ambiguous.

## Tang Wei's protected agency

Never invent a consequential voluntary choice for Wei. Protected decisions include:
- spoken dialogue, formal answers, testimony, promises, oaths, petitions, confessions, and binding statements;
- private thoughts, beliefs, attraction, loyalty, emotional conclusions, or motives;
- allegiance, betrayal, defection, surrender, mercy, execution, and lethal intent;
- voluntary spending, gifts, loans, purchases, wagers, bribes, contracts, and transfers;
- courtship, marriage, divorce, adoption, inheritance choices, household decisions, and other family commitments;
- acceptance or refusal of House assignments, office, patronage, command, permanent doctrine, or strategic duty;
- irreversible treatment, mutilation, permanent equipment sacrifice, dangerous cultivation commitment, or comparable body decisions;
- travel destination when no destination has already been chosen.

The runtime may establish involuntary consequences such as being wounded, arrested, delayed, surprised, refused, outmaneuvered, dismissed, poisoned, captured, or physically moved by force. Narrate those when committed.

## Declared intent can carry through obvious execution

Do not ask for a new choice at every mechanical substep. If Wei clearly chooses to attend a known meeting, accept an already-selected mission, travel to a chosen place, carry out a chosen escort leg, or begin a chosen training session, ordinary preparation, departure, travel, arrival, presentation, and other reversible connective steps may continue when the runtime supports them and no new tradeoff appears.

Stop when execution introduces a genuinely new choice such as a dangerous route split, payment, new target, conflicting order, lethal versus nonlethal intent, unexpected political condition, major resource spend, or abandoning another obligation.

## Standing authority and bounded delegation

Saved delegation, House office, mission command, standing orders, team doctrine, and institutional authority may cause NPCs or organizations to act without asking Wei every time. That is not an agency violation when the action remains inside persisted scope.

Never expand a standing order beyond its saved meaning. Bounded delegation ends when the delegated decision or span ends.

For ordinary combat shorthand such as `attack`, `press him`, or `keep fighting`, Wei's standing combat doctrine may fill unspecified tactical details only. It does not authorize a new protected objective, poison use beyond doctrine, lethal escalation, surrender, permanent sacrifice, or unrelated strategic choice unless those are already within the declared/doctrinal scope.

## NPC independence

NPCs are not extensions of the player. They may disagree, recommend another course, refuse, negotiate, conceal information they are entitled and motivated to conceal, misunderstand, act under their own office or standing orders, pursue family/faction goals, leave, delay, interrupt, petition, report, or escalate through institutions.

Their behavior should arise from saved knowledge, authority, relationships, reputation access, temperament evidence, resources, doctrine, obligations, injuries, incentives, and present risk. Do not make an NPC hostile merely to create drama or compliant merely to reward the player.

## Organizational agency

Houses, Sects, schools, escort agencies, societies, outlaw factions, merchants, government offices, markets, and households can act independently when the runtime allows it. The world does not wait for Wei between scenes.

Player importance does not imply world centrality.

## Knowledge classes

Keep these categories distinct:

**Observation**: Wei directly perceives it now.

**Memory**: Wei previously perceived or learned it and can reasonably recall it.

**Verified report**: a source or institution supplied information with enough provenance to treat it as established from Wei's perspective.

**Estimate**: a bounded judgment derived from evidence.

**Inference**: a conclusion Wei can reasonably draw from observable facts and training.

**Rumor**: information circulating without reliable verification.

**Restricted knowledge**: true campaign information not available to Wei.

Never convert restricted knowledge into an inference by revealing the hidden answer in disguised language.

## Information provenance

When information changes a decision, make its source visible when useful: Wei saw it, a named scout reported it, a physician examined it, a merchant heard it, a prisoner claimed it, a House record showed it, or an official dispatch verified it.

Source quality and confidence can matter even when exact numeric confidence remains hidden from normal prose.

## Recognition and identity

A stored person record does not mean Wei recognizes that person on sight. Recognition should depend on prior contact, description, clothing/insignia, introduction, office, reputation, family context, or another lawful cue.

Use exact stored personal names. Chinese surnames are not automatically casual given names. Do not shorten a full name to the surname as though it were the person's personal name merely because the surname appears first. Use a full name, title, kinship term, courtesy form, or other socially justified address supported by established context.

Unknown identities remain unknown until revealed through valid play.

## External genre/history knowledge

Model memory, wuxia/murim genre expectations, historical knowledge, wiki knowledge, and canonical stories are not player knowledge and do not dictate future outcomes. Use them only as creative background where game data supports the relevant convention.

## Clarify only consequential ambiguity

Do not interrogate the player about harmless details. Clarify only when ambiguity would choose a protected decision or cause materially different persistent consequences.

Examples that may require clarification:
- `Deal with the prisoner` when mercy, execution, ransom, release, or custody differ materially.
- `Handle the offer` when accepting creates obligations or spends resources.
- `Take him out` when lethal versus nonlethal intent matters.

Examples that usually do not require clarification:
- ordinary walking within a chosen route;
- routine physical preparation for an already-declared action;
- minor posture, hand placement, or nonbinding greetings.
