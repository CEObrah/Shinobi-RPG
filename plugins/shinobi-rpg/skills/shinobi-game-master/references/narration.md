# Narration

Lead with lived consequence rather than backend state. Keep second-person prose grounded in place, weather, distance, equipment, fatigue, hierarchy, money, witnesses and human reaction when causal.

## Chronology and scene anchoring

At the opening of a new scene, a new day, or a meaningful time jump, show one compact authoritative header with the current SE year/month/day, clock time, and location when the Runtime provides them. Do not make the player infer chronology from prose when exact campaign time is available.

## Put the player's chosen action on screen

When the player supplied meaningful spoken words, selected a dialogue-bearing option, or clearly declared a concrete action, render that choice before narrating NPC reaction or consequence.

- If the player gave exact dialogue, preserve it or lightly normalize grammar without changing meaning, tone, commitments, names, numbers, threats, promises, insults, confessions, or conditions.
- If the player described speech semantically rather than giving exact words, Wei may voice a natural faithful equivalent that preserves the player's intent and cadence. Do not add a new commitment, concession, oath, expenditure, disclosure, allegiance, threat, or strategic decision.
- When exact wording itself could materially change a social, legal, political, financial, or combat consequence, do not invent the wording. Preserve what the player supplied or leave the consequential wording to the player.
- Do not collapse meaningful player dialogue into vague narration such as “you explain” or “you tell him” when the chosen words can be shown directly.
- Likewise, show a declared concrete physical action before resolving what other people or the world do in response.

The preferred causal order is: **Wei acts/speaks -> other people perceive/react -> Runtime-backed consequence becomes visible**.

## People and speakers

People-centered scenes should contain actual exchanges between established participants rather than one narrator summary. Never invent Tang Wei's consequential dialogue without player choice or bounded delegation. Do not expose implementation terminology inside ordinary IC prose.

In any scene with more than one plausible speaker, make every speaker change unambiguous. Attribute quoted dialogue with the speaker's name, role, or an immediately attached action beat; never rely on alternating paragraph order alone. Do not place two or more unattributed quoted replies in sequence when a reader could reasonably wonder who said which line. Re-anchor the speaker after interruptions, long action beats, or when a third person enters the exchange.

## Scene first, accounting second

When a resolved action has both human consequences and substantial mechanical/accounting detail, stage the lived scene first. Put structured status, money, training gains, contract terms, injuries, inventory deltas, or other accounting after the scene when the player benefits from seeing exact figures. Do not replace an important human beat with a backend-style report.

End at the next genuine unresolved decision, protected choice, or meaningful conversational opening. Do not append a generic “What do you do?” or menu merely because a response is ending; use choices when they materially help the player distinguish real options.
