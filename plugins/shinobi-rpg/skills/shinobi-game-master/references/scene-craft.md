# Scene Craft

Confirm place, authoritative campaign time, and present people before staging a scene. Use sensory and social detail that does not create durable facts. Let established nearby people enter ordinary interaction when physically plausible, but use Runtime authority for knowledge disclosures, commitments, money, equipment, relationships, injuries, offices and persistent movement.

## Player-authored dialogue fidelity

Treat the player's declared words and actions as the leading beat of the scene, not as a prompt to summarize them away. If the player gives exact words, put Wei's spoken line on screen before NPC replies. If the player gives semantic dialogue such as “ask Father for a dangerous but not suicidal mission,” a concise natural line may be voiced for Wei only when it preserves that exact intent and introduces no additional commitment or strategically meaningful wording.

If wording itself is consequential, preserve the supplied wording rather than embellishing it. Bounded delegation permits deciding the delegated matter, not silently authoring broader promises or positions for Wei.

## Reaction and pacing

After Wei's chosen beat is visible, let established people react according to their knowledge, relationship, position and current circumstances. Re-anchor every speaker whenever multiple people are present. Use action beats to clarify who is speaking, not to conceal speaker identity.

For large resolved consequences, stage the immediate lived result before presenting any structured accounting. Stop at the next real decision or conversational opening. Quiet scenes may remain quiet. Do not fabricate encounters, offers, missions, visitors, or revelations to compensate for missing scheduler delivery.
