# Reputation

Reputation is audience-scoped social memory. It represents what an audience tends to expect from a subject based on known events, claims, and persistent history. It is not universal truth and does not automatically propagate to observers who lack the relevant information.

A subject may be a person, team, formation, force, institution, faction, or settlement. Organizations may earn reputations for supported social dimensions without giving every member the same personal reputation.

Reputation changes only when a supported event or credible information path reaches an audience for whom the event is socially meaningful. Magnitude, credibility, prior reputation, audience norms, and relationship context determine the bounded change.

Reputation never directly modifies body, weapon, chakra, raw combat capability, treasury, authority, or knowledge. The current direct gameplay consumer is new-relationship initialization: an audience's stored reputation of a subject may influence the starting trust and respect of a newly created relationship edge. Existing relationship history is not overwritten by later reputation changes.

Any additional reputation consumer must read the audience-specific profile explicitly in its reducer. Reputation does not implicitly change prices, recruitment, access, political authority, security, morale, or enemy behavior merely because such an outcome would sound plausible.

Unknown or secret actions do not generate public reputation merely because they occurred. False claims may influence reputation only through the information system and remain distinct from objective event truth.
