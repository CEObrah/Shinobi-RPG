# Large-Force Resolution

Mass combat resolves through **formations**. A formation is an operational allocation of personnel from one force. Its internal **components** separate personnel when troop type, operational role, condition, rank mix, or registered initiative tendency must remain distinct for aggregate computation or conservation.

A formation's headcount includes every exact named person embedded inside that formation. Named people and exact teams are not additional manpower. When four named members of an exact team are operating inside a 500-person formation, they occupy four of those 500 positions; the remaining 496 may remain aggregate.

Keep named actors exact whenever identity, technique, command, injury, relationship, objective, or unique equipment can materially change the result. Their bounded exact contribution applies without adding bodies, and an exact combat zoom may reserve them from anonymous casualties when opposing exact actors exist.

Aggregate capability is derived by the runtime from each component's troop type/role and the matching force capability source, weighted by component headcount, then modified by formation readiness, morale, cohesion, initiative tendency, supply/front state, fortification, command, and lawful exact overlays. The caller does not choose a favorable troop-pool profile.

Split aggregate representation only when a distinction can change a registered outcome or must be conserved. Do not create components merely to preserve descriptive differences that no reducer uses.

Scale changes computation only. A 50-person formation and a 5,000-person formation use the same underlying laws. Larger formations use distributions and deterministic aggregate kernels for efficiency, not a representation bonus.

A force's personnel partition and its formations must conserve headcount. Split, merge, movement, casualties, capture, recovery, reconstitution, reinforcement, and release may redistribute represented personnel but may not create or erase them.
