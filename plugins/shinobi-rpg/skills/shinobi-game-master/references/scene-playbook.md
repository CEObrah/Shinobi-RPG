## Select the pressure, not the wallpaper

Choose scene technique from the actual causal pressure. A scene in the Hokage Tower may be social, covert, administrative, political, or an emergency command scene. A scene in a training yard may become medical, relationship-focused, or combat-critical.

Use the runtime-returned narration module as the cold scene-specific hint. Use this playbook to render the scene with the Skill's core voice.

Use fresh `scene_cast` before treating a people-centered scene as empty. Immediate presence comes from `present_people`; `nearby_people` are established locally but not automatically in the room; `referenced_people` are relevance only. When `scene_vitality` permits ephemeral motion, a nearby established person may enter or leave the immediate interaction when the local geometry makes that ordinary. This is scene staging, not permission to create a persistent location change or other durable fact.

Use ordinary background activity when it helps make an established place feel inhabited. Unnamed clerks, guards, servants, trainees, laborers, customers, relatives, or civilians may perform obvious routine functions under `scene_vitality`, but do not turn them into named persistent people, exact staffing counts, new authorities, sources of information, or hidden plots.

Do not preload every scene style. Apply only what changes the current rendering.

## Social and political scenes

Make hierarchy, audience, relationship, reputation, and authority visible through behavior.

Track who is present, who may speak first, who can interrupt safely, who can commit resources or policy, who needs to save face, what is public versus private, what each participant actually knows, what procedure constrains action, and what prior promises or reputation shape reception.

Let pauses, wording, honorifics, seating, documents, witnesses, side glances, private requests, and who is excluded carry social information.

Render conversation as conversation. When named people are present and able to speak, show actual NPC lines rather than paraphrasing an entire exchange. Let dialogue reveal hierarchy, uncertainty, disagreement, familiarity, face-saving, incomplete knowledge, and relationships without turning every scene into a speech.

A nearby known person may enter the interaction for ordinary conversation when `scene_vitality` permits it. Before the exchange becomes consequential, use the targeted person sheet if voice, knowledge, relationship, health, authority, or commitments materially matter. A greeting can be ephemeral; a disclosure, promise, order, refusal with institutional consequence, or relationship change is not.

Before a material line, account for both speaker and addressee. Age, rank, role, prior relationship, audience, and current purpose should change wording and behavior when established facts support it.

Do not reduce politics to hidden villainy. Competing legitimate interests, limited resources, status, procedure, fear, loyalty, family, and institutional survival can create pressure without a conspiracy.

Do not force every conversation toward a persuasion roll. Sometimes the meaningful outcome is information gained, a boundary clarified, a relationship changed, an obligation created, a refusal recorded, or a future meeting established.

## Crowded-cast scenes

When several named people share a scene, keep the player oriented without converting prose into a roster dump.

- Re-anchor an infrequently seen or easily confused person with a compact player-known role cue on first meaningful appearance.
- Do not repeat the cue every time the name appears.
- Attribute dialogue explicitly whenever three or more speakers could plausibly own the line.
- Make addressees clear when direction changes meaning.
- Let NPCs interact with each other, not only with Wei.
- Group background actors by function and zoom into exact names when individual behavior matters.
- Preserve different ages, ranks, professions, personalities, relationships, and authority lines in how people interrupt, defer, joke, correct, challenge, report, and disagree.
- Do not silently convert every `nearby_people` entry into immediate presence. Bring in only the people whose local movement is plausible and useful to the scene.

The player should be able to answer `who is this, what are they doing here, and who are they talking to?` without consulting old chat turns.

## Covert and investigation scenes

Prioritize information quality and exposure.

Keep distinct what is observed, what might be inferred, what remains unexplained, what evidence exists, who could have left it, how fresh it appears if supportable, who has seen Wei, what traces Wei's actions may leave, and relevant routes, sight lines, sound, lighting, cover, routines, locks, seals, patrols, and surveillance.

Never invent a clue because the player searched well. Let the runtime determine whether evidence exists and what was learned.

Presentation latitude does not apply to evidence creation. An incidental background action may make a place feel occupied, but it may not produce a suspicious document, overheard secret, revealing witness statement, hidden watcher, security gap, or other investigatory fact unless the runtime establishes it.

Avoid fake investigation prose such as `you sense there is more here` without a concrete observation.

When multiple explanations fit the evidence, preserve them. Do not collapse uncertainty into the hidden answer.

Compress routine searching once the meaningful method is established. Expand discoveries, detection risk, contradictions, access changes, pursuit, and evidence handling.

## Mission scenes

Keep the mission objective and constraints present without constantly restating a mission card.

Track issuer and authority, objectives, deadline, classification, participants and roles, known intelligence, civilians or protected assets, extraction conditions, evidence, route/location, and mission-changing events when relevant.

Do not assume mission success means every secondary objective succeeded. Do not assume mission failure means every action was useless.

When circumstances change, make the changed objective or constraint legible before asking the player to decide.

Routine team chatter, equipment handling, and movement inside an already-established mission scene may use presentation latitude. Mission intelligence, hostile presence, route hazards, principal status, evidence, objective progress, and mission outcomes may not.

## Training and development scenes

Treat training as embodied practice, not a stat montage.

Establish training objective, instructor or leader, participants, doctrine or curriculum, facility/equipment, intensity, known time available, and fatigue or injury constraints.

Compress repetition after the pattern is clear. Expand recurring errors, correction, coordination problems, tactical misunderstandings, breakthroughs, injury or overtraining, relationship changes, doctrine becoming physically understood, member divergence, and hard interrupts.

Do not narrate mechanical growth before it is committed. Do not turn a single good repetition into permanent mastery unless the runtime did so.

For team training, show shared familiarity through spacing, timing, signals, role transitions, trust, and exploitation of openings rather than floating statistics. Let participants sound like a team in motion: tactical callouts, questions, corrections, confirmations, terse disagreements, and debrief remarks. When several named NPCs materially participate, normally surface at least two distinct NPC voices before compressing the drill unless speech is causally suppressed. Attribute those lines clearly. Never supply Wei's dialogue.

Presentation latitude may supply harmless drill texture, banter, corrections, water breaks, equipment checks, and local movement. It may not create an injury, breakthrough, relationship change, new technique, equipment loss, or persistent training gain.

## Travel and pursuit scenes

Compress safe, repetitive movement. Expand what changes route, time, risk, knowledge, resources, relationships, or mission state.

Keep route, party, terrain, pace, causal weather, encumbrance/injuries, checkpoints, borders, patrols, settlements, deadlines, arrival conditions, and pursuit/evasion geometry relevant when they matter.

Do not add random encounters merely to make travel interesting. The world can be quiet.

For pursuit, shift toward combat-like spatial clarity: relative position, route options, obstacles, visibility, exhaustion, separation, and objective.

If the player has already chosen a known destination as part of a broader declared action, routine travel is a prerequisite step, not automatically a new choice. Resolve it through the proper runtime command unless route or timing creates a material decision.

Presentation latitude cannot move Wei, a party, or an NPC between persistent locations. It applies only to local staging inside the already-established location or route situation.

## Downtime and recovery scenes

Allow quiet play to be genuinely quiet.

Meals, shopping, paperwork, equipment care, recovery, baths, sleep, household life, inspections, ordinary training, waiting, and casual conversation do not need artificial danger.

Use downtime to make the world human and persistent: people have routines, equipment needs handling, institutions create paperwork, injuries require care, relationships develop through repeated contact, obligations occupy time, and absence from one responsibility can matter elsewhere.

This is where presentation latitude should do the most work. If the place and local cast support it, let teammates cross paths, household members carry on routine tasks, workers handle equipment, trainees clear a yard, a clerk chase a signature, food cool on a table, or a nearby known person stop in for a brief ordinary exchange. None of those require a persistent event until they create a real decision or durable consequence.

Do not confuse repeated casual contact with an automatic relationship increase. Let the scene provide human continuity, while relationship state changes only through the appropriate runtime mechanics.

Compress uneventful stretches while preserving time and material consequences.

Do not turn every quiet scene into foreshadowing.

## Family and relationship scenes

Treat family, friendship, mentorship, rivalry, romance, household, and clan ties as human relationships before ledger changes.

Build relationships through accumulated evidence: remembered actions, reliability, disagreement, affection shown behaviorally, boundaries, status and family expectations, shared work, conflict, humor, obligation, absence, and repair after harm.

Dialogue should change with intimacy, age, family role, culture, privacy, and unresolved history. A parent, sibling, spouse, child, mentor, rival, subordinate, and old friend should not receive the same social posture from the same NPC.

Presentation latitude may support ordinary conversation and familiar behavior consistent with an existing relationship, but it cannot deepen affection, establish attraction, grant forgiveness, create a promise, or infer consent merely because the prose is warm.

Do not jump from one conversation to deep intimacy. Do not narrate another person's consent, proposal acceptance, forgiveness, loyalty, or family decision unless committed state supports it.

Never supply Wei's romantic or family consent.

## Clan and house scenes

Treat a clan compound, house estate, district, dojo, council room, household wing, shrine, archive, kennel, workshop, or training ground as part of a living institution rather than decorative scenery.

Establish only player-visible facts, but when they exist keep track of:

- whose authority governs the place;
- clan head, elders, branch leaders, household heads, heirs, retainers, servants, guards, instructors, and dependents who materially matter;
- who is family, subordinate, guest, outsider, allied shinobi, political visitor, or village official;
- age and generation, including how elders, adults, youths, and children are treated differently;
- main/branch or other internal divisions when the campaign actually establishes them;
- public rooms versus private household space, restricted archives, secret training areas, and ordinary work areas;
- hospitality, visiting protocol, seating, privacy, ceremony, meals, gifts, escorts, guards, and who may overhear;
- property, training, business, marriage, succession, reputation, village duty, and inter-clan politics when causal.

Do not portray everyone in a clan as sharing one voice or ideology. Let individuals differ by personality, age, household, branch, office, profession, personal relationships, ambitions, grievances, and experience. Clan culture should shape behavior without erasing individual character.

Make clan life social. Relatives speak to each other. Elders interrupt juniors. Children and trainees exist when supported. Staff and guards have work. Specialists may argue within their domain. Household members may have shorthand, private jokes, resentments, affection, hierarchy, or old history that differs from how they behave before outsiders.

When `scene_vitality` permits unnamed routine background roles at a confirmed clan seat, use them to show work and domestic life without inventing exact clan population, named relatives, branch offices, security staffing, political factions, or persistent relationships. Named consequential clan actors still require authoritative support.

Do not reduce a famous clan to its signature ability. An Uchiha scene is not automatically about Sharingan; a Hyuga scene is not automatically about Byakugan; an Inuzuka scene is not automatically about ninken. Profession, family, village service, age, ordinary domestic life, and personal history matter too. Never grant a technique, bloodline expression, or secret because of surname alone.

Clan politics should remain grounded in actual authority and interests. The clan head cannot automatically command every member in every village office if the persisted authority model says otherwise. Village law, mission command, clan authority, family obligation, and personal relationships can overlap or conflict.

When Wei enters another clan's space, make his relationship to that clan matter if established: rank, reputation, friendship, marriage ties, mission history, favors, political standing, or outsider status may affect access and wording. Do not invent deference or hostility merely because Wei is important.

If a clan is narratively relevant but the runtime lacks enough institutional detail to support consequential leadership, branches, property, resources, or politics, keep those facts unknown and flag the missing clan depth OOC rather than filling the gap from model memory.

## Command and institutional scenes

Show that authority is a system, not a magical power.

Track what Wei is authorized to order, who receives the order, what resources are controlled, which subordinates have delegated discretion, competing duties/deadlines, readiness/personnel/equipment/information/location, procedure, reports, approvals, and political consequences when relevant.

A lawful order does not guarantee perfect execution. A subordinate can exercise initiative within authority. An institution can continue operating while Wei is elsewhere.

Orders have receivers. When named subordinates are present, show them acknowledging, clarifying, questioning, reporting a constraint, or otherwise responding according to age, rank, relationship, knowledge, temperament, and stakes when material. Do not reduce a live command scene to narrator summary while everyone around Wei remains silent.

Subordinates may also speak to each other. A deputy can correct an operative, a specialist can challenge an assumption within their expertise, and teammates can coordinate without waiting for Wei to mediate every exchange.

Use presentation latitude for routine institutional life such as paperwork moving, runners carrying ordinary messages already represented by the scene, stores work, equipment checks, shift changes, or staff waiting on a decision. Do not let an ephemeral clerk approve policy, create a mission, allocate scarce resources, reveal restricted information, or exercise authority that the runtime did not establish.

When presenting strategic choices, distinguish immediate command actions from wider policy or planning choices.

## Large-scale war scenes

Narrate at formation and command scale unless the runtime crosses into exact-person resolution.

Use fronts and sectors, terrain, routes and crossings, formations and reserves, objectives, command relationships, morale and cohesion, readiness, casualties, supply/support when mechanically relevant, information delay when established, and civilian or political consequences.

Do not narrate a thousand-person formation as a collection of invented individual soldiers.

When named actors become causal, zoom in cleanly. When their exact exchange ends, reconcile and zoom back out without double-counting effects.

Presentation latitude may animate an already-established command post with generic messengers, map handling, equipment, or staff motion, but may not create new reports, orders, casualties, unit movements, or intelligence.

## Scene transitions and appointments

Mark meaningful changes in time, place, pressure, or authority with a clean transition. Do not over-label routine cuts.

At an arrival, establish the new smallest useful frame rather than summarizing the entire journey.

At a hard interrupt, state what interrupted the prior plan and how much of that plan actually completed according to runtime state.

Do not narrate an abstract administrative phrase where a lived transition is clearer. Avoid lines such as `You choose Fujin` or `the scheduler clears` in normal fiction. Show the concrete consequence: Wei leaves Sword Manor, crosses Konoha, reaches the reporting place, finds the team waiting, or discovers that he is late, only when those facts are mechanically committed or player-visible.

For a declared appointment or report-for-duty intent:

1. identify the known reporting place and due time;
2. compare them with Wei's current place and world time;
3. resolve required departure/travel before claiming attendance;
4. do not claim punctuality, lateness, or arrival until the runtime establishes it;
5. if ordinary logistics introduce no meaningful choice, carry them through as part of the declared intent;
6. if route, timing, danger, cost, or conflicting duties create a material tradeoff, stop at that decision;
7. after arrival or the next boundary, refresh context and continue the scene.

At a genuine decision point, stop before Wei acts and use `references/choices.md`.
