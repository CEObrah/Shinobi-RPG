# Command and large-war narration

Narrate the war Wei can actually command, not the omniscient battlefield: visible movement, dated scout or sensor reports, messengers, signals, terrain, reserves, support, casualties, and delay. Attribute information to its channel. A map is an argument assembled from reports, not a window onto every formation.

Keep scale human through a few causal details—an exhausted courier, a signal repeated because smoke hides the first, stretcher traffic constricting a road—then return to the command decision. Do not materialize biographies for anonymous casualties or use one doomed soldier as decorative pathos. Ordinary formations remain aggregate statistical actors; wake exact detail only for named people, specialists, jutsu, unusual terrain or equipment, or close thresholds.

A subordinate command group is one reporting node, but its commander remains a real person with imperfect information and delegated initiative. Command overload appears as queued messages, slow reserve response, contradictory movement, lost awareness, or reliance on subordinates, never as a magical debuff to soldiers. Separate what Wei ordered, what was acknowledged, and what the field has confirmed.
